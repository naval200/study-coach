Concept checks

The coach asks questions to find what you really know.

Four depth levels

LevelYou can…
1 awareSay what it is
2 explainExplain it without notes
3 applyUse it, build with it, or fix it
4 teachHandle edge cases and trade-offs

Your level is the highest level that you did without help. To read, to watch, or to say "that makes sense" is not proof.

How a check works

  1. You and the coach agree on a target level.
  2. The coach asks 3–5 questions, one at a time.
  3. The coach gives no hints.
  4. Before it shows the answer, it asks: "sure or unsure?"
  5. It grades your answer.
  6. It stops when you reach the target, or when you miss two questions in a row.

Grades

SureUnsure
Correctsolidfragile
Wrongmisconceptiongap

A partly correct answer counts as wrong. A skipped question counts as a gap.

The final grade is the worst grade at the target level.

Review schedule

A script sets the date of the next review. It uses these steps (in days):

1 → 3 → 7 → 16 → 35
  • solid: move up one step.
  • fragile: stay on the same step.
  • misconception or gap: go back to step 1.

After the last step, the concept is "consolidated".

Due reviews

The dashboard shows concepts that are due. The coach offers them as a warm-up before your first block. It asks 1–2 questions for each concept. It does a maximum of 5 concepts.

Errors that occur again

The coach records errors in concepts/_misses.md. If you make the same error again, it adds one to the count. There are two types:

  • confusion: you mix up A and B. The coach asks "A or B?" questions.
  • trap: you make the same wrong step. The coach makes a new case with the same trap.

Are you ready?

Before a test or interview, the coach puts concepts in three groups:

  • Reliable: solid at the target, checked recently.
  • Fragile: fragile, or solid only one time.
  • Untested: never checked. This does not mean "probably good".
