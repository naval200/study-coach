# Making the plan stick

A plan survives when a bad day has a small, clear next step. Build these in with the learner, in their words.

## Size to real hours and energy
Plan to the hours they kept up last time, not the hours they hope for. If unsure, take 70% of what they say. Fit blocks to their real session length and to weekday vs weekend time, and ask how intense a week they can sustain for months. A plan that is finished early can grow at a checkpoint; a plan that is behind from week 1 dies.

For experienced learners, a slower, lighter plan is often better: retention and application matter more than syllabus speed.

## One unit, one rhythm
Pick one unit of progress the learner can count (a lesson, a chapter, a Build task) and a steady number per week. Show pace as "about N per week, done by <date>". Offer Relaxed / Steady / Faster with the finish date for each.

## Minimum day
Write the 20-minute version: e.g. "one exercise, or 15 minutes of the day's Learn, then write tomorrow's first task". It counts. It keeps the chain alive without guilt.

## Rules (the learner signs off on each)
1. **Never shift the calendar.** A missed day's Build moves to the light day; its Learn drops unless the Build needs it.
2. **Time boxes are hard.** When time is up, commit and stop. Leftovers go to the light day.
3. **Stretch items drop first** when behind.
4. **New courses, books and videos go to the parking lot**, never into a block. They get looked at during review.
5. **Two missed days in a row → scope cut**, not extra hours.
6. **Judge by the exit test**, not by hours logged.

## Pre-mortem
Ask: "It is week 3 and you have stopped. What happened?" For each answer write an if-then rule:
- "If I travel for work, then I do the minimum day from the hotel and skip Build."
- "If I find a better course, then it goes to the parking lot until Sunday's review."
- "If a Build task runs over 2×, then I cut it to the smallest working version and move on."

## Fixed time
Put each block at a fixed time in the day (a run-sheet). Decide the first task of tomorrow at the end of today.

## Visible proof
Each week ends in something others can see: a repo, a number, a post. It makes progress real and gives the next week a starting point.

## Checkpoints
Fix the dates the plan may change (e.g. Day 30, 60, 90). Between them, changes go through study-coach `adapt`: each addition names what comes out.

## Language
No "behind", "failed" or "streak broken" in the plan. Missing is information, and the rules say what to do.
